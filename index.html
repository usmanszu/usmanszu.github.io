Research Overview

3D Skeleton-Based Human Action Recognition · Self-Supervised Contrastive Learning · Geometry-Aware Representation Learning

My research focuses on self-supervised contrastive representation learning for 3D skeleton-based human action recognition. I investigate how human-motion representations can be organized across multiple granularities, how discriminative joints can be selected and transferred across models of different capacities, and how complementary geometric, spectral, and semantic information can improve representation learning.

My work combines hierarchical contrastive learning, joint-aware knowledge transfer, geometric algebra, geometry-aware modeling, spectral motion analysis, language-guided representation learning, graph neural networks, and spatiotemporal Transformers to develop robust, efficient, and transferable representations of human motion.

Research

I study how the structure and dynamics of articulated motion can improve representation learning.

01

Hierarchical Contrastive Representation Learning

Coordinating global and local skeleton representations across multiple temporal and spatial granularities.

02

Joint-Aware Learning and Knowledge Distillation

Selecting discriminative joints and progressively transferring knowledge across representations of different capacities.

03

Geometric, Spectral, and Semantic Representation Learning

Integrating geometric-algebra-based latent interactions, temporal spectral information, and language-derived semantic guidance.

04

Geometry-Conditioned Motion Representation

Using explicit motion-aware joint–bone geometry to condition temporal and spatial representations for action recognition.
